### Master-Thesis
Optimization of Production Process


Abstract - This thesis delves into the optimization of the production process in the field of adaptive optics, aiming to enhance overall efficiency through an evaluation of various production steps that directly influence effeciency. The study thoroughly investigates the factors contributing to inefficiencies and explores a range of potential solutions to address these challenges. Furthermore, it provides a detailed examination of the design, development, and practical implementation of the identified solutions to ensure meaningful improvements in the production process.

A significant aspect of this research involves the establishment of a quality control checkpoint specifically designed for the inspection of membrane surfaces. In adaptive optics, product quality is influenced by the high-reflective glass substrate, the membrane it is bonded to, and the precision of the bonding process. As the shape of membrane have influenced over optical abberations like astigmatism, coma etc. By integrating this checkpoint into the production workflow, the study aims to enhance quality assurance measures, thereby minimizing defects and ensuring consistent product standards. The implementation of this quality assessment mechanism plays a crucial role in maintaining high-performance output while reducing waste and rework.
Additionally, this thesis presents an in-depth analysis of the outcomes observed after the implementation of the proposed solutions. By comparing pre- and post-implementation performance metrics, it provides valuable insights into the effectiveness of the optimization strategies employed. ### Working Principle.

 A piezoelectric actuator plays a crucial role in dynamically adjusting the shape of the membrane, which serves as the foundation for the attached mirror. This adjustment is achieved by applying a controlled voltage input, which induces a mechanical deformation in the actuator due to the piezoelectric effect. As the voltage varies, the actuator expands or contracts, causing precise modifications in the membrane’s surface profile. These adjustments enable real-time control over the mirror’s curvature and alignment, making the system highly responsive to operational requirements. Such fine-tuned control is particularly essential in applications requiring high-precision laser machining, ensuring optimal performance and minimizing optical distortions.

### Conclusion 


To conclude, the introduction of surface quality checkpoints both prior to and following the piezo altern process has demonstrated significant promise in enhancing the overall efficiency of the manufacturing workflow. This strategic approach not only streamlines the production process but also contributes to a noticeable reduction in material wastage. Based on the evaluation of 25 ZWOBBEL systems, it became evident that incorporating an early-stage quality inspection could have effectively filtered out units with suboptimal surface characteristics before they advanced to more resource-intensive stages, such as piezo altern and mirror gluing. This would have translated into substantial savings in terms of both production time and high-cost materials.
